Ini jawabannya, simple English kelas 8: **Station 1: Life on the Homefront** 1. Newspaper accounts were inaccurate because news traveled slowly and rumors spread fast. The most accurate information came from letters from soldiers. 2. Mourning rituals included wearing black clothes, holding funerals, saving letters, and keeping pictures or objects from the person who died. 3. Women had to run farms and plantations while men were away fighting. They planted crops, took care of animals, and managed the home. 4. Women and children near battles had to leave their homes, hide, or deal with damage. Some also helped wounded soldiers. 5. People helped soldiers by sending food, clothes, blankets, letters, and medical supplies. 6. A famous Union spy was Harriet Tubman. 7. After 1863, many enslaved people in Tennessee became free or escaped to Union lines. --- **Station 2: Amputations** 1. If a soldier had an amputation within 48 hours, his odds of survival were better. 2. Chloroform and ether were commonly used for anesthesia. 3. The instruments were surgical saws, knives, scissors, forceps, and clamps. 4. The prosthetics industry grew because many soldiers came home missing arms or legs. 5. Similarity: Both pictures show artificial arms. Difference: One is an older Civil War prosthetic, and the other is a more modern prosthetic arm. --- **Station 5: Prisoners of War** 1. The South refused because they did not want to treat Black soldiers as equal soldiers. 2. Bellows Island Prison camp later became a park or public land after it was filled. 3. Three hazards were disease, lack of food, and dirty water. 4. A stockade smelled because many prisoners were crowded together with bad sanitation and human waste. 5. Other than physical suffering, the hardest thing would be fear, loneliness, and not knowing if you would survive. **Words to Know Part 2** Hardtack: a hard cracker eaten by soldiers. Casualty: a person killed, wounded, missing, or captured in war. Skirmish: a small fight or battle. Flank: the side of an army formation. Artillery: large guns or cannons used in war. --- **Station 6: Women of the War** Pakai **Harriet Tubman**. 1. She aided the Union, or the North. 2. She helped the war effort by working as a spy, scout, and nurse. 3. She gathered information for the Union, guided people to freedom, and helped care for wounded soldiers. 4. She faced danger, arrest, and possibly death if she was caught. **Words to Know Part 3** Emancipate: to set someone free from slavery. Greenbacks: paper money used by the United States. Goobers: peanuts. Hard Knocks: difficult experiences or hard lessons in life. Hunkey Dorey: everything is fine or okay. Skedaddle: to run away quickly. --- **Station 7: 54th Massachusetts Infantry** 1. Frederick Douglass helped recruit African Americans to the army. 2. Robert Gould Shaw was chosen to lead the 54th unit. He was chosen because he was a white officer from a respected family and supported the cause. 3. They made their famous stand at Fort Wagner near Charleston. About 40 percent were casualties. 4. About 180,000 African Americans joined the war effort in the North. 5. William Harvey Carney became the first African American awarded the Medal of Honor. 6. The memorial is located in Boston Common. --- **Station 8: Daily Life of a Soldier and Desertion** 1. Uniforms were usually made of wool. Union uniforms were blue. 2. Many soldiers slept on the ground because tents were crowded, heavy, or not always available. 3. Soldiers played cards, wrote letters, sang songs, read, told stories, and played games. 4. Confederate uniforms were usually gray or butternut. Soldiers were often missing shoes, blankets, food, and proper clothing. 5. They were similar because both sides had hard lives, poor food, disease, and long marches. They were different because Union soldiers usually had better supplies, while Confederate soldiers often lacked supplies. 6. The average pay of a Union soldier was about $13 a month. African American soldiers were first paid about $10 a month, with $3 taken out for clothing. 7. Many people deserted because army life was dangerous, food was bad, pay was low, and they missed their families.
